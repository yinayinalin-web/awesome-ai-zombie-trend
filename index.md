## What the trend actually is

Almost every viral clip tells the same tiny film: a bleak, colourless now in which one of the two is already undead; a small object (a ring, a bracelet, a collar) that triggers recognition; a hug instead of an attack; and a cut to a warm, sunny memory. Most clips run 10 to 25 seconds and are vertical 9:16.

## Three ways to make one

| Route | What you do | Good for |

| General AI video model | Upload reference photos, write the prompt, often join several clips | Full control over every shot |

## Prompt patterns

If you take the general-model route, these habits matter more than any single magic prompt:

1. **One action per shot.** "She lifts her head and sees the bracelet" works; three actions in one shot usually blur.
2. **Name what makes a face recognizable**: hair, glasses, a jacket. Ask the model to keep those identical across shots.
3. **Describe the zombie as movie makeup**: grey-green skin, cloudy eyes. Many models refuse blood and wounds, and the trend does not need them.
4. **Render the undead scene and the sunny memory separately**, then join them; one prompt for both tends to mix the lighting.
5. **Say who turns.** With two people in one photo, models often swap them unless told.

Ready-to-paste versions, written out one shot at a time, live in these [shot-by-shot prompt templates for the zombie trend](https://zombietrendai.org/zombie-ai-prompts).

## Photo habits that keep faces right

- One clear, front-facing photo per person beats a group shot.
- Crop tight: a head-and-shoulders frame survives the animation far better than a face snapped across a room.
- Skip sunglasses, masks and heavy beauty filters; the model copies what it sees.
- Recent photos give the closest result; faces come out close to the photo, not as perfect copies.

A longer version of these checks, with what to expect from each kind of tool, is on this [photo checklist for keeping your face in zombie and hug videos](https://sites.google.com/view/zombie-video-photo-checklist).

## Consent and privacy

- Stick to your own face, grown-ups who said yes, or an animal that lives with you.
- Leave famous people, politicians and passers-by out of it entirely.
- Before uploading, check how long the tool keeps your photos and whether it lets you delete them.
